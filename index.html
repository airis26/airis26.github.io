ABOUT THE CONFERENCE

What is NC-AIRIS'26?

The First National Conference on Artificial Intelligence: Research, Innovation & Systems (NC-AIRIS'26) is a premier event organized by the Faculty of Exact Sciences at the University of Mascara in partnership with the LABTEC AI laboratory.

This conference brings together leading researchers, industry experts, and policymakers to explore cutting-edge AI advances and shape the future of intelligent systems in Algeria.

The event features keynote lectures, technical sessions, panel discussions, and networking opportunities focused on emerging AI technologies and their societal impact.

Keynote Talk

Strengthening Brain-Computer Interfaces Against Adversarial Threats: From Exposed Weaknesses to Robust AI Solutions

Abstract

Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs) are rapidly advancing neurotechnology by enabling direct communication between the human brain and intelligent systems. Despite their transformative potential, BCI platforms remain highly vulnerable to security threats, particularly adversarial attacks targeting their underlying machine learning models.

This keynote examines the emerging adversarial threat landscape in BCI systems, emphasizing how such attacks can undermine signal reliability, system performance, and user safety. The talk critically analyzes the shortcomings of conventional deep learning approaches and how more sophisticated architectures, including Capsule Networks and attention-driven models, can enhance robustness.

Recent empirical results on adversarial resilience in BCI applications are presented, alongside promising defense mechanisms such as adversarial training and GAN-assisted data augmentation. By integrating perspectives from artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, and neuroscience, this presentation advocates for interdisciplinary strategies to develop resilient, secure, and trustworthy BCI technologies.

Keynote Talk

Artificial Intelligence as a Tool for Research and Innovation

Abstract

This presentation provides a transformative framework for integrating artificial intelligence into scientific discovery, shifting researchers' perspective from viewing AI as a simple shortcut or replacement to utilizing it as a controllable 'cognitive multiplier'.

It gives researchers a clear, progressive roadmap moving from structured prompt engineering techniques to grounded document synthesis (Retrieval-Augmented Generation [RAG] and Cache-Augmented Generation [CAG] architectures to eliminate hallucinations) and autonomous agentic workflows that automate complex, multi-step literature reviews.

Ultimately, it equips academics with actionable strategies to drastically scale their research productivity and hypothesis testing, while maintaining rigorous human oversight and neurosymbolic verification.

Submission Guidelines

All submissions must comply with the following guidelines:

  • Full paper submission only: Abstracts are not accepted separately
  • Paper length: 6 pages
  • Language: English
  • Anonymization: Papers must be anonymized for review
  • Format:
    • Title: Bold, 14 pt, Times New Roman
    • Text: 12 pt, Times New Roman, 1.5 line spacing
  • Additional Requirements:
    • Maximum of 5 authors
    • Include 5–6 keywords
    • Indicate the corresponding subject areas.

Revision Note: Revised papers (for accepted papers) must follow a specific template, which will be provided later. Authors must reveal their names and affiliations in the revised version.
